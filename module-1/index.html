Framework 01

Isolate the variable

Most issues look bigger than they are because several things are tangled together. Isolating means changing or testing one factor at a time so you know exactly what's responsible — instead of guessing at the whole system at once.

Probe tip

When a customer says "it just stopped working," ask what changed right before it stopped — a new device, a settings change, an update. Whatever changed is your first suspect.

Framework 02

Identify the pattern

One odd ticket is an anecdote. Several similar tickets are a pattern — and a pattern points you toward a shared cause. Before treating a report as one-off, check whether it correlates with timing, device, plan tier, region, or user role.

Probe tip

Before you troubleshoot a single ticket in isolation, search for others like it. "Has anyone else mentioned this?" often does more diagnostic work than any single question you could ask the customer.

Framework 03

Verify assumptions

"It doesn't work" can mean five different things. Customers describe symptoms in their own words, and their mental model of the system isn't always accurate. Confirm the specifics diplomatically before you act on them.

Probe tip

Ask the customer to walk you through exactly what they clicked, in order, and what they saw at each step. Precision beats politeness here — a gentle "can you walk me through it step by step?" surfaces the gap fast.

Framework 04

Uncover the root cause

Fixing a symptom feels productive, but it doesn't prevent the ticket from coming back. The "Five Whys" technique keeps you drilling past the visible symptom until you hit the actual mechanism behind it.

Probe tip

If your fix could be undone by the customer simply doing the exact same thing again, you probably solved the symptom — not the cause.

Case study

The case of the vanishing invoices

Put all four frameworks to work on one ticket, step by step.

"Hi — over the past two weeks, some of our invoices just disappear from the dashboard a few days after we create them. It's happened four times now. We need this fixed, our finance team is worried we're losing records."
Step 1 of 3 — Choose your opening probe

Customer's reply: "Invoice #2291 was created on the 3rd. I noticed it missing on the 6th. It happened to three other invoices too — all of them were created more than 3 days before they vanished."

Step 2 of 3 — What do you isolate next?

What you find: The account has an auto-archive rule that moves invoices older than 3 days out of the default dashboard view into an "Archived" tab — working exactly as configured, just not as expected.

Step 3 of 3 — What's the root cause?

Case closed

Nothing was lost — the invoices were exactly where the system's rules put them. Isolating the timeframe (Framework 01), noticing the 3-day pattern across four tickets (Framework 02), verifying "vanished" actually meant "moved" rather than "deleted" (Framework 03), and tracing it to the archive rule instead of stopping at "it's a bug" (Framework 04) turned a scary-sounding ticket into a two-minute settings fix.
