Section 3

Validate the customer before you explain or resolve

People don't just want the right answer — they want to feel heard first. Here's the sequence that makes both possible.

A common customer-service mistake is jumping to the solution too quickly. An employee may know exactly what needs to happen — but if the customer feels emotionally ignored, even the correct explanation can land badly. Empathy should generally come before explanation or resolution.

🧭 The four-step path

Tap each stop to see what it sounds like in practice.

1
Recognize
Name the feeling
2
Acknowledge
Say it out loud
3
Explain
Give the "why"
4
Act
Show what's next

🔍 Spot the difference

Same situation, two responses. Which one actually helps?

Response A
"I understand. Unfortunately, that's our policy."
😕 Feels like a dead end
Response B
"I can see why that would be frustrating. Let me explain what I can do from here."
🙂 Feels like forward movement

Same policy. Very different landing.

Response A moves straight from a generic "I understand" into a policy statement. What the customer often hears is: "I heard you, but there's nothing you can do."

Response B recognizes the customer's emotional experience first, then establishes forward movement. The phrase "Let me explain what I can do from here" is doing the real work — it bridges empathy into action.

Notice what it doesn't do: it doesn't promise an outcome the employee can't guarantee. It just communicates genuine willingness to help.

Remember the path:
Recognize→ Acknowledge→ Explain→ Act
